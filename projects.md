## Data Centers Next Door {#data-centers}

**Tools:** Python, R, Shiny, GeoPandas

**Key Questions:**
 - What is the associated impact of data centers in Chicago on housing prices, household costs, and broadly cost of living? 

![Data Centers Next Door](/images/datacenters_next_door.png)

[View Project Website](https://rodrigofrancac.shinyapps.io/project-datacenter-urban-effects/) or [View on GitHub](https://github.com/uchicago-2026-capp30122/project-datacenter-urban-effects)

This project explores the cost-of-living implications of data centers in the Chicago metro area. As data centers are known to exacerbate electricity and water costs and housing prices, we wanted to map those patterns at a local level in the Chicago metro area. Our project takes zip code-level housing price and utility/household costs data and converts it into a composite index score, and finds the average composite score associated with a zip code before and after a data center was built in that zip code. This is for 45 datacenters built between 2000 and 2020 in the Chicago metro area. We built a dashboard showing the average impact score of datacenters on cost of living (+5.5), with a map visualization allowing for comparisons of housing prices and costs between zip codes in the Chicago metro area.

## Federal Disaster Relief & Income Inequality {#disaster-relief}

**Tools:** Python, Pandas, Plotly, R (dplyr), Stata, IPUMS, FEMA OpenData

**Key Questions:**
- Does federal disaster relief increase or decrease income inequality?
- Which regions and states are most affected by large disaster aid disbursements?

![Data Visualizations From Senior Thesis](/images/ThesisScreenShotMoreDetail.png)

[View Project](/disaster-relief)

This project examines the relationship between federal disaster relief and income inequality across all 50 U.S. states from 2005 to 2019. Using FEMA disaster aid data, IPUMS income distribution data, and miscellaneous control data, I constructed a state-year panel dataset and estimated a partial adjustment model (AR1 process). The analysis finds that federal disaster relief is associated with an increase in the Gini coefficient — a standard measure of income inequality — suggesting that aid disproportionately benefits higher-income households in the aftermath of disasters.
